My research focuses on **assessing forest carbon stocks by combining satellite data with deep learning**. During my PhD (2020–2023, Université Paris-Saclay) I developed methods combining Sentinel-1/2 imagery, GEDI LiDAR and deep learning to map forest height, wood volume and biomass across France: the [FORMS](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/4927/2023/) maps.

As a postdoc I extended this work in two directions: to **annual time series** revealing year-to-year forest growth ([FORMS-T](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2025.114959)), and to **very-high-resolution (1.5 m) tree-level monitoring** of forest disturbances from SPOT 6/7 imagery ([FORMSpoT](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425726004013)). Together these open the way toward an annual carbon balance for forests using satellite imagery alone.

I collaborate with researchers across several institutions on concrete applications: the 2022 French wildfires, agroforestry stocks in Italy, and reconstructing the Ukrainian forest inventory from satellite data during wartime. I am also interested in bringing new sensors (ESA's BIOMASS mission, CNES's CO3D) into these models, and in making my data useful to civil society.
